# Crafting Plugin

## Purpose

The **Crafting** app (`/campaigns/:id/crafting`, sidebar "Crafting", behind
the `crafting` addon and shown only while the Armory is on). A Director writes
recipes, tags Armory items as materials, sets up benches and picks the
campaign's crafting defaults; a player reads the recipe book of what their
characters know. A player starts a project from the **Crafting block** on
their character's page, paying the materials and cost at the start, and makes
one roll for every respite the Director logs, at the bench or as a plain
roll; the Director follows every project on the app's **Projects** tab.
What a fight or a search leaves waits in the party's **spoils tray**: players
take or harvest it from the block, and the Director hands it out, puts more
in and keeps every page's **drop list** on the **Spoils** tab. An AI reads
all of it and writes recipes (with their rules), material kinds, drop lists
and benches through AI Import; see "The AI connection".

The bench itself (`static/js/widgets/craft_bench.js` and its parts) and the
"When… then…" rules editor (`craft_rules.js` and its parts) are core widgets
with their own `.ai.md`; this plugin stores what they edit and mounts them
through their `el._craftApp` hook.

## Who may do what

| | Recipe book | Editors' data (recipes, catalog, benches) | Any change | Projects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Player | approved recipes everyone knows, plus those one of their characters learned | no (403) | no (403) | start, roll, cancel, experiment, break down and help for a character they claimed; any other character is a 404 |
| Director (Owner or granted co-Director) | every approved recipe | yes | yes | all of that for any character, plus the Projects tab, respites, giving a roll and setting points |

Spoils follow the same split: a player takes, harvests or studies a waiting
line for a character they claimed (any other character is a 404); the Spoils
tab, drop lists, putting spoils in, clearing them, revealing a find and
giving to any hero are the Director's.

Every route is open to any member (`RequireRole(RolePlayer)`); the service
decides with `Viewer.Director` (`CampaignContext.CanAuthorDmOnly()`), which a
role middleware cannot express. Each Director-only method starts with
`directorOnly(v)`, so a mis-wired route cannot widen access.
`TestEveryWriteIsDirectorsOnly` calls every write as a player and checks
nothing changed.

## What a player never receives

The book is built on the server (`Service.Book`, `book.go`); the browser only
draws it.

- Drafts are left out.
- A rule reaches the book only when it is written in the book
  (`players: "book"`); rules shown when they happen and secret rules never do.
- Item names come from the items the player may see (`Directory.ListItems`
  at their role). An item they cannot see reads "an item you haven’t seen".
- `knownBy` names only the player's own characters.
- A recipe on the Crafting block carries only the rules written in the book.
  When a rule holds a roll or changes one, a rule shown when it happens is
  named; a secret rule reads "A rule the Director keeps hidden", never its
  condition. What a project took and whether its item reached the pack are
  the Director's only.
- A roll carries only the How it looks moment that happened (`rollMoment`:
  a row of the recipe's own mishap table, then breakthrough, success,
  failure for botched, spoiled or lost work, else progress) and the
  Director's look for it, so a player never sees the words of a mishap that
  didn't strike. The block shows the words in the result and, with motion
  on, plays the look over it (`craft_moment_look`, on the rules editor's
  stage). A rule's own look still plays only in the editor (TODO(#1989)).
- The bench opens in the player's look (`pickSkin`): the one they switched
  to with the link under the bench, kept in the browser per campaign; else
  the Journal on a screen 640px wide or less and the Director's look wider.
- The block renders only for the Director and the player who claimed the
  character (`CanCraftFor`), so nobody else's page carries an empty block.
- A roll reads the calendar's today with the Director's sight, because the
  rules are the Director's (ADR-049's allowlist, `crafting_adapters.go`).
  The block shows today's date only to a viewer who can see the default
  calendar, and a respite's date is on the Director's tab only.
- An experiment never names a recipe before it is found: a near miss
  answers with the recipe's hint only, never its name or id, and a recipe
  with "Finding it by experimenting" off is never matched, hinted at or
  counted. The block's "Try something new" shows only while there is
  something this hero could still find.
- The party's projects list another hero's projects only when the recipe
  lets others help and the hero is a claimed character the viewer can see;
  what a project took stays the Director's.
- A drop list never leaves the Director's routes. In the tray
  (`trayViews`), a spoil whose page the player can't see reads "Something
  the party found", with no copy count, no fight name and no "put in by
  hand"; an item they can't see reads "An item you haven’t seen" with no id
  or kinds, until a hero takes it and the Armory lets that hero's player see
  it. The tray names the page, never the name the Director typed on the
  fight's row, and the pack's history never names where a spoil came from.
- A find marked for study that nobody has identified reads "Something
  unidentified" to a player, with no item id, tags or kinds, and a harvest's
  test and amounts left out; the server refuses a player who tries to take
  it. Who studied it and what they rolled is named only for a hero the
  player can see ("Another hero" otherwise).

## Making recipes where the work is

Every way below makes an ordinary crafting recipe, which the app, the item's
page and the book show like any other. Each arrives as a draft unless the
Director said otherwise.

- **An item's page.** The `crafting_item` block ("How it's made") renders on
  item pages for members. Nothing joins a page layout by itself, so the
  Director places it once from the recipe editor's list ("Show 'How it's
  made' on item pages", `POST /item-block`), which adds it after the entry
  of every item category's layout. A Director sees the recipes that make the
  item, edited in place, and for a material its kinds, the recipes that use
  it and the drop lists that give it. A player gets only the book pages they
  can already read and uses in approved recipes; an item they can't see is a
  404, and the block hides itself when it has nothing to say.
- **The Workshop's Crafting recipes part.** Part kind `crafting-recipes`,
  drawn by the `crafting-recipes-part` widget. Its data is `{who, start,
  recipes}`: who can make them (`all`, `learned`, or `director`, which is
  learned with nobody taught), whether new rows start as drafts, and the
  recipe ids it holds; a deleted recipe drops out on the next save. The
  action `crafting.teach` teaches the party a recipe by name.
- **The generator** (`POST /generate`). Seeded: the same seed, inputs and
  rerolls give the same rows, and a reroll changes one row. Rows are built
  from the campaign's own items by kind and the bench's methods. A goal comes
  only from the system's published `crafting.goals` (by echelon and what is
  made, then by echelon), else the median of the Director's approved
  recipes, else none: the Director types it before keeping. Weapons and gear
  have no bench steps. Nothing is saved until the Director keeps rows; kept
  rows are drafts and make their result item when none is named like them.
- **The AI door.** The part builds a prompt naming the campaign's materials
  and methods; the paste goes to the AI page's own parse route with
  `review_host=hbw-ai-review-host`, so the review and its checks are the
  usual ones (owner only). Recipes the commit made join the part.
- **Recipe packs.** A pack is a JSON body (`format: chronicle-recipe-pack`,
  `version: 1`) holding recipe names, methods, steps, goals and words, and
  for materials only their names and kinds. It never carries an item id, a
  page's text, a character or who learned a recipe. A Director reads a pack
  of this campaign, of another campaign they direct right now, or one
  shared on the server; anything else is a 404. An uploaded file is checked
  in full (`decodePack`: format, version, names, kinds, lines pointing at the
  pack's own materials, the limits of 200 recipes, 600 materials and 1 MB).
  Bringing one in matches each material to an item here or makes it new as a
  Director-only item, and makes drafts; a name clash or a refused recipe is
  skipped with its reason.

## Data model

| Table | What |
|---|---|
| `crafting_settings` | one row per campaign: default look, difficulty, botched work gives a bane, players may skip steps |
| `crafting_recipes` | a recipe; materials, steps, rules, looks and extras are JSON cleaned on every write; name unique per campaign |
| `crafting_known` | who knows a "must be learned" recipe, one row per character |
| `crafting_material_tags` | a crafting tag on an Armory item |
| `crafting_kind_pictures` | a campaign's picture and tint for one kind, when it isn't the default |
| `crafting_material_pictures` | an item's own picture and tint, when it doesn't follow its kind |
| `crafting_benches` | a named bench of one kind, with each look's build-mode arrangement (`desk`, `journal`) and its upgrade ladders |
| `crafting_projects` | a hero's project: what the recipe said when it started (name, goal, characteristic, item), points, rolls ready, status, whether the item reached the pack, what it took, and its details (what the item came out as, the substitutes' standing costs) |
| `crafting_respites` | one row per respite logged, with the calendar's date then |
| `crafting_rolls` | every project roll: dice, each term, the total, points before and after, how the work went |
| `crafting_turns` | a hero's try or hand since the latest respite, one row each, so a new respite gives every turn back |
| `crafting_drops` | a page's drop list: its lines (JSON, cleaned on every write), the page it also drops from, a version |
| `crafting_spoils` | one thing that dropped: the page and its name then, which copy of how many, the fight's name ("" by hand) |
| `crafting_spoil_lines` | a spoil's line as the drop list read then: item, take or harvest, amounts, whether it needs study (with which characteristic, one chance or not); waiting, taken (by whom, for which hero, how many, what was rolled), cleared or lost |
| `crafting_known_items` | an item the party has identified: by which hero (none when the Director revealed it), and whether that study also showed the recipes that use it |
| `crafting_studies` | every study and reveal: item, line, hero, outcome, the roll, and the respite it came after |

- A material's item id is not a foreign key: a deleted item leaves the recipe
  saying "That item was deleted" rather than changing what it asks for. The
  result item is a foreign key that sets NULL; deleting a character removes
  it from `crafting_known`.
- A stored tag the library no longer has is dropped on read (`knownTags`), so
  it never hides the item's other tags.
- Pictures are named for what they show (`leaf`, `flask`, `ingot`), never for
  a material; the drawings are `static/js/widgets/craft_pictures.js` and
  `Pictures` in `library.go` lists the same keys in the same order
  (`TestPicturesMatchTheDrawings`). An item wears its own picture, else its
  first kind's as the campaign set it, else that kind's default. A stored
  picture the library no longer has falls back the same way.
- An item's first tag picks its picture and recipes store tag keys, so new
  kinds are appended to `Tags` and keys are never renamed
  (`TestKindOrderIsAppendOnly`).
- Armory items are entity types with `preset_category = 'item'`, the
  Armory's own rule. A campaign without a game system that adds item types
  has no items to tag.
- A project copies the recipe's name, goal, characteristic and item when it
  starts, so editing or deleting the recipe never moves the goal under a
  player; the recipe's rules still apply while it exists.

- Deleting a page deletes its drop list; a list that also dropped from it
  stops doing so, and a spoil from it keeps the page's name. A spoil line's
  item cascades: an item deleted from the Armory leaves the tray.

## Spoils and harvesting

- **What drops.** Ending a fight on the DM Screen's Fight section tells
  `FightEnded` which creature rows were down: every copy at 0 hit points or
  less drops its own share of its page's drop list, the page it also drops
  from first (one step only). A row with no page, a page with no drop list,
  or a creature not down leaves nothing, and the report says which. The
  encounters plugin ends the fight once (`EndRun` reads and deletes in one
  transaction), so two presses can't drop twice. The Foundry module's
  end-of-combat prompt reaches the same `FightEnded` through the sync API
  once the GM confirms; each of those calls drops again, so the module sends
  one per combat. **Put spoils in the tray** drops a page's list by hand, up
  to 20 times, for a search or a fight run elsewhere.
- **Copied at drop time.** A spoil's lines are the drop list as it read
  then; editing the list later changes only what drops next. Spoils keep
  until taken or cleared.
- **Taking** claims the line with one conditional update (first come, first
  served), then the Armory puts it in the hero's pack (`GiveSpoil`). If the
  pack refuses, the line goes back to waiting.
- **Harvesting** is a test: the system's dice plus the characteristic the
  line names, read from the hero's sheet (+0 with a note when the sheet has
  no number for it). The system's tiers (Draw Steel: 11 or less, 12 to 16,
  17 or more) each pay what the line says, and the lowest pays at least 1.
- **Studying.** A drop line marked **Needs study first** lands in the tray
  locked: players read "Something unidentified" until a hero studies it, a
  test of the system's dice plus the line's characteristic (the editor starts
  at Reason, or Intelligence). The lowest tier is a miss; the middle one tells
  the whole party what the item is; the top one also names the recipes that
  use it, of those the studying hero can see in the book. Once identified,
  an item is known to the party for good, wherever it drops again.
- **Who studies when.** A hero who missed an item may study it again after
  the next respite the Director logs; another hero can try at once. The
  study's row is written before its dice (`StartStudy`, one conditional
  insert against the latest respite's id), so a hero can't roll twice
  between respites, even with two presses.
- **One chance.** A line marked One chance is lost on its first miss: it
  leaves the tray for everyone (status `lost`) and nobody learns what it was.
- **The Director's side.** The Director sees every find by name, can
  **Reveal to the party** without a roll, and Give or Harvest for them hands
  a locked find to a hero without a study. Every study and reveal is listed
  under Taken lately.
- **The tray is capped** at 500 waiting lines; a fight that would overflow
  it drops nothing more and says so.
- **The Fight tab** asks `DropNotes` for a chip beside each creature ("Has a
  drop list", "No drop list") and shows none when no page in the fight has
  one. With crafting off, or a listener that fails, the fight still ends.

## The AI connection

AI Import (and the connector's `crafting` tool) reads crafting through the
`crafting` lookup and writes it with four blocks, all in
`internal/plugins/ai_workspace/records` (`crafting.go`, `crafting_kinds.go`,
`lookup_crafting.go`) over `aiCraftingAdapter`, so every write goes through
this service's own checks:

- **How the parts tie together.** A material is an Armory item with one or
  more kinds (`kind: material`, which calls `SetTags`). A recipe asks for it
  by the item's name or for any item of a kind (`kind: recipe`). A creature,
  Remnant, place or magic source gives it through its drop list
  (`kind: drops`), and a fight's end drops that list into the tray; a line
  ending `; study with Reason` (and `, one chance`) needs a study first. The
  lookup prints each material with the pages that drop it and the recipes
  that use it.
- **A recipe from an AI arrives as a draft** (`SaveAIRecipe`), so players
  see nothing until a Director approves it in the editor. Changing a recipe
  keeps it approved or not, keeps its rules, looks, colours and bench, and
  keeps each step on the same material (`KeepStepMaterials`) unless the AI
  sends `method`, which lays the steps again with `LayTemplate`, exactly as
  the editor's `applyTemplate` does.
- **Rules ride on the recipe block** as the rules editor's AI form
  (`ruleData`), printed by the lookup one per line. `MergeAIRules`
  (`ai_rules.go`) reads the whole list: a rule sent back exactly as stored
  keeps its approval and look, any other arrives with `draft: true`, which
  the verdict and the roll ignore until a Director approves it, and a rule
  `cleanRule` can't read refuses the save with its place in the list (the
  editor's own cleaner drops it quietly). The rules are written after the
  recipe with `UpdateRules` at the version that write made, so an edit in
  between is a conflict.
- **A `bench` block** makes a bench or changes its name and kind through
  `CreateBench`/`UpdateBench`; arrangements and upgrades stay the
  Director's, and removing stays on the Benches tab.
- **The review runs the save's checks without saving** (`check`), the name
  included, so the review and the commit refuse the same things. An item
  the same paste makes is checked once its page exists.
- **Directors only** for every block and every part of the lookup but the
  recipe book; a lookup in the Safe privacy mode reads as a player and gets
  only the book. While the add-on is off, AI Import refuses the four blocks
  (`aiFeatureGate`) and the lookup says crafting is off.
- The tray, projects and how a bench is arranged are changed only in the
  app.

## Projects

- **Starting.** The hero must know the recipe (approved; known to everyone
  or learned). The service checks the materials and the cost against the
  hero's pack, then the Armory takes them all or nothing (`Armory.Spend`). A
  Draw Steel hero's Wealth must reach the cost and is never spent; coins are
  converted to the sheet's money field. Each "any herb" line is filled with
  the item the player picked.
- **Rolls.** Each respite adds one roll to every project in progress; unused
  rolls stack. A roll is made on the server: the system's dice, the
  characteristic from the sheet, an edge for clean work and a bane for
  botched work (when the campaign gives banes), then the recipe's rules on
  the day. The browser only reports how the steps went (`clean`, `botched`,
  `steady`, `skipped`), so the most it can claim is an edge. Skipping is
  refused when the campaign asks for the steps and the recipe has some,
  unless a Director rolls or the hero has mastered a recipe whose mastery
  gives a skip (the player's recipe carries `skip` so the block offers Just
  roll).
- **The day's rules.** `verdict.go` reads the rules the way the rules editor
  previews them: a certain "can't be made" or "only then" with none on holds
  the roll (409, the roll stays ready); a chance of "can't be made" is rolled
  and spoils that roll; points, an edge or bane, a say line and extra items
  are added. The roll knows the bench, the time of day by the calendar's
  clock and the maker's skills from the sheet. Ingredients are the copies
  the project took: a rule needing a mark is on when a copy with that mark
  went in, and with the recipe's Quality setting fine and poor ingredients
  add their edge, bane or points. The start fold offers each remembered kind
  of a material by name (gone-off ones never), and `StartInput.Use` sends the
  copies picked; plain ones fill the rest.
- **What befalls the work.** A fired rule can make the item fine, mark it,
  give it a quirk rolled on one of the campaign's Rolling Tables, or make it
  as another Armory item; these are kept on the project and shown when it is
  made. "Discovers…" teaches the maker that recipe. The recipe's "If it goes
  wrong" settings add a mishap rolled on a table, at its chance, and what
  botched work costs (a bane, a quirk, or the work's points). Its extras add
  a breakthrough on a natural high roll, mastery (an edge once the hero has
  made it cleanly often enough) and substitutes the hero picks when
  starting, each with its standing cost. A substitute names its material by
  position, so saving a recipe's materials moves each substitute with its
  material and drops one whose material is gone (`FollowMaterials`). A table or item the campaign
  doesn't have is told to the Director only.
- **Bench upgrades.** Each piece of a bench climbs a ladder of up to five
  levels; a level is words for the table or a rule in a recipe's words. The
  rules of every level the party has reached join the recipe's at every roll
  made at that bench.
- **Finishing.** Reaching the goal makes the item (points over the goal are
  dropped) and the Armory puts it in the pack. The made item remembers its
  quality, marks, enchantments and quirk, the maker's mark when the recipe sets it, and,
  with a calendar, the day it goes off and what it then becomes; the result
  card lists them. A harvested spoil remembers its fine or poor cut and who
  took it. At each respite the Armory turns what went off (`GoOff`); if
  that or the respite's Homebrew rules fail, the respite still counts and
  its reply carries `warnings` the Projects tab shows. A gone-off copy is
  never spent as a material or broken down (`craftPicks` in the Armory's
  `stash_craft.go`). If that fails the project
  still finishes, marked undelivered, and the Projects tab tells the Director
  to give it by hand.
- **Enchanting.** A recipe whose extras set `enchant` (`{line, name,
  slots}`) gives back the item that went in instead of making one: one copy
  of material `line` goes in (the remembered copy the player picked, else a
  plain one; with every copy remembered and none picked, the start asks for
  a pick) and comes back as itself, with what it remembered plus the
  enchantment `name`, keeping its own picture, maker and shelf life. The
  start refuses a copy that already carries the enchantment or holds
  `slots` of them, before anything is taken. The recipe's own result item
  is not used, and a breakthrough's extra never copies the item. The copy
  as it was is kept on the project (`Details.Base`, `BaseItem`).
- **Cancelling** keeps what the project took; the Director gives things back
  by hand. An enchanting project is the exception for its item: cancelling
  gives that one copy back as it was.
- **Experimenting.** Once at each respite a hero puts things from their
  pack into a try. Each recipe the hero could still find (approved, "must be
  learned", not learned by them, "Finding it by experimenting" on) counts the
  materials the try got right: the right item, or one with the right tag, in
  the right amount, each thing counted once. All right with nothing extra
  finds the recipe: it teaches the maker or, when the recipe says so, every
  claimed character, and uses up what went in. Right on at least the
  recipe's "k of n" is a near miss that shows the hint. Otherwise the nearest
  recipe's failed-try cost applies (all, half of each line rounded down, or
  nothing), and with no recipe near at all everything is used up. The turn
  is taken first and given back if the Armory refuses.
- **Breaking down.** A hero who knows a recipe with "Breaking it down" and
  holds what it makes can break one down, the remembered copy they pick or
  any: it leaves the pack and the salvage lines come back. A line names an
  item by name, matched first against the recipe's own materials, then any
  Armory item; a line naming none (an "any herb" slot) is noted for the
  Director, and when no line names an item the break-down is refused and
  the item kept. No roll and no turn.
- **Helping.** A hero can lend a hand on another claimed character's project
  in progress when its recipe lets others help, once for each project at
  each respite. The hand is one extra plain roll at the campaign's first
  bench of the recipe's kind with its upgrades (none of that kind is a
  plain roll), adding the helper's characteristic and
  skills, never their mastery; the points are the owner's, the owner's ready
  rolls stay, and a finished item goes to the owner's pack on the helper's
  behalf (`Armory.GiveFor`).
- **At the bench.** With motion on, the block plays each of these at the
  campaign's bench (`craft_moments.js`), then shows the panel under it;
  under Calm, Off or reduced motion the panel is all there is. The bench's
  scripts load when the moment mounts it, and a bench that can't draw, or
  hasn't ended the moment in 20 seconds, shows the panel anyway.
- **Respites** come from the Projects tab (which then runs the Homebrew
  rules a respite sets off) or the Homebrew drawer's "Log a respite" button
  (whose `Pressed` hook logs it here first). Either way one press is one
  respite.
- **Versions.** A roll, the Director setting points and cancelling carry the
  project's version; a stale one is a 409 and the browser reloads.

## The system's crafting rules

A game system package may declare a read-only `crafting` block in its manifest
(`internal/systems/crafting.go`): the dice, the characteristics a recipe may
add, what an edge and a bane are worth, the harvesting tiers, and the words
for the goal, prerequisite and source. A campaign with no block, or no system,
gets `DefaultCraftingRules`. `Library` sends the rules filled in, so the
editor never checks for gaps. A recipe's `stat` is a characteristic key from
the block, or free words when the block names none.

## Saving in the browser

Everything saves as the Director works; there is no Save button.

- **Recipes**: one queue per recipe. The form and the rules save on their own
  routes, so the recipe form and the rules editor never overwrite each other;
  each save waits for the one before it and carries the version it returned.
- **Benches**: one queue per bench. A rename goes first, then the upgrade
  ladders, then each look's arrangement; `then(fn)` waits for the queue before the bench remounts.
- **Material tags** save per row on each click and roll back on failure.
- **Drop lists** save a moment after the last change, one save at a time per
  page, each with the version the last one returned.
- **Pictures** save on each pick; a swap on the picture board is one save.
- Whatever is still queued is sent with `keepalive` when the page closes.
