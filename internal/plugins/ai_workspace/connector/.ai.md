# ai_workspace/connector

## Purpose

Lets an AI app (Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Open WebUI, LM Studio) connect
straight to one campaign over the Model Context Protocol, look things up and
ask for changes, with nothing to copy and paste. No tool changes the
campaign: a request for changes waits on the owner's Your AI page
(`ai_workspace/ai_page.*`) until they review it on the paste's review screen
and press Apply, so an AI app set to "Allow always" still applies nothing.
The owner sees every call there as "What it did".

| `repository.go` | SQL for `ai_connections`, `ai_connection_calls` and `ai_connection_requests` |

| `sittings.go` | `Sittings`: the call log grouped by quiet gaps of more than 30 minutes, with counts per kind of call |
| `handler.go` | `Endpoint` (`POST /api/ai/campaigns/:id/mcp`; GET and DELETE answer 405) and `RegisterRoutes` |

## The key

- `chai_` plus 32 random bytes in base64url. Only its SHA-256 is stored
  (`key_hash`, unique) with its last four characters (`key_hint`). It is
  shown once, in the `ConnectAI` response (`Cache-Control: no-store`).
- Sent as `Authorization: Bearer …` or `X-API-Key`. Never in the address,
  never a cookie: the endpoint is under `/api/` so the CSRF check skips it,
  which is safe only because nothing there reads a session.
- One owner, one campaign, one privacy mode and one Change setting
  (`can_ask`). The owner can change both, or pause the key (`paused_at`),
  on the same key (`ChangeAccess`, `Pause`, `Resume`: owner checked, and
  only the campaign's current live key, named by id). `Authenticate` reads
  the row on every call, so a change applies from the next call; a paused
  key is refused with 403 "This key is paused", which does not count as a
  wrong key. A campaign
  has one connection at a time: `Connect` revokes earlier ones and stores
  the new one in one transaction holding the campaign's row
  (`Repository.Replace`).
- Lasts `KeyLifetime` (90 days). `Authenticate` refuses it once revoked,
  expired, presented at another campaign's address, or when its maker's
  account is disabled (`FindByHash`) or no longer owns the campaign
  (`Owners.Check` on every request; a deleted or trashed campaign fails it).

## Answers

`actorFor` reads a Safe connection as a player and a Permitted one as the
owner, the copy-and-paste lookups' `exportActor` rule. Each tool is one
`records.Lookups.Answer` call, so privacy, the size caps (24 KB an answer)
and the page-walk limits are the lookups' own. `campaign_outline` is
`Lookups.PageIndex` (8 KB). `initialize` tells the AI the tools only read
and that everything they return is the campaign's writing, never
instructions. Every tool is annotated `readOnlyHint: true`.

## Asking for changes

Only a connection made with "Ask for changes" lists the two extra tools.

- `how_to_write_changes` is the copy-and-paste prompt's standing
  instructions (`ai_workspace.Handler.ConnectorInstructions`: every block
  format, the rules, the campaign's kinds of pages and sheet fields, none of
  its content), prefaced with what differs over a connection.
- `ask_for_changes` takes `summary` (one line, 200 characters) and `changes`
  (the blocks, at most 256 KB and 200 blocks, lines kept). `Service.Ask`
  parses them as the review will and refuses, with the reason, a block that
  won't parse, a lookup or a part label, so the owner is never left a broken
  request; it names at most 5 problems and counts the rest.
  Then the campaign check (`SetCheck`, wired to
  `ai_workspace.Handler.ConnectorCheck`) refuses what the AI can fix itself,
  such as a new page filed in a folder its category doesn't have; a check
  that fails is logged and skipped, since the review checks again. A
  good one is stored in `ai_connection_requests` with Chronicle's count
  ("2 to add, 1 to change") and nothing else happens. The AI is told nothing
  has changed. Annotated `readOnlyHint: false`, `destructiveHint: false`.
- At most 20 requests wait per campaign (`Repository.AddRequest` counts and
  inserts in one transaction holding the campaign's row); the 21st is
  refused until the owner answers one. Answered ones are pruned to 100.
- The owner's side (`WaitingRequest`, `Answered`, `TurnDown`) only ever
  reads or changes a waiting request of their own campaign. Requests stay
  after a disconnect: they are the owner's to answer.

## Limits

- No per-address limit in front, since hosted AI apps share addresses.
  Refused keys are limited per address (20 a minute, then 429 without a
  log line each); a good key always gets through. A key of the wrong shape
  never reaches the database.
- Per connection: 30 calls a minute, 2000 a day, in memory (429 with
  `Retry-After`).
- A request body is at most 576 KB (room for a 256 KB request for changes,
  escaped); one message per request (no batches); every other argument is
  at most 200 characters and only a tool's own arguments are read.
- A request with an `Origin` other than the site's own is refused (403).

## The call log

One row per `tools/call` and one "Connected" row the first time an app
connects: when, tool, a one-line summary of what was asked (the AI's own
words in quotes, so they never read as Chronicle's), a note (the
answer's size, or why it wasn't answered). Never the answer itself, and for
a request for changes never the blocks: "Asked for 2 changes: “…”". 500 rows
are kept per campaign (`CallsKept`); the page groups them into sittings.
