# ai_workspace/connector

Lets an AI app (Claude, Claude Code, ChatGPT, Open WebUI, LM Studio) connect to one
campaign over the Model Context Protocol, look things up and ask for changes. No tool
changes the campaign: a request for changes waits in the AI panel of the person whose
key it came with (`ai_workspace/ai_panel.*` for the owner, `ai_workspace/ai_player.*` for
a player, opened from the AI button on any page) until they review it and press Apply,
so an AI app set to "Allow always" still applies nothing. The owner sees their own key's
calls on the Your AI page (`ai_workspace/ai_page.*`) as "What it did"; a player's calls
and requests are the player's, and the owner never sees them.

## The key

- `chai_` + 32 random bytes in base64url. Only its SHA-256 is stored (`key_hash`,
  unique) with its last four characters (`key_hint`). Shown once, in the `ConnectAI`
  response (`Cache-Control: no-store`).
- Sent as `Authorization: Bearer …` or `X-API-Key`; never in the address or a cookie.
  The endpoint is under `/api/`, so the CSRF check skips it, which is safe only because
  nothing there reads a session.
- One person, one campaign, one privacy mode, one Change setting (`can_ask`). The
  person can change both, or pause the key (`paused_at`), on the same key
  (`ChangeAccess`, `Pause`, `Resume`: only their own current live key, named by id). `Authenticate` reads the row on every call, so changes apply from the next call;
  a paused key gets 403 "This key is paused", which does not count as a wrong key.
- One live key per person per campaign: `Connect` revokes that person's earlier keys
  (never anyone else's) and stores the new one in one transaction holding the
  campaign's row (`Repository.Replace`). Every read of keys, calls and requests is
  scoped to the person (`user_id` through `ai_connections`).
- Lasts `KeyLifetime` (90 days). `Authenticate` refuses a key that is revoked, expired,
  presented at another campaign's address, whose maker's account is disabled
  (`FindByHash`), or, for an owner's key, whose maker no longer owns the campaign
  (`Owners.Check` on every request; a deleted or trashed campaign fails it). A player's
  key is checked as described under "Players' keys".

## Answers

`actorFor` reads a player's key as that player (role Player, their own user id, so
their own claimed and shared pages and nothing else), a Safe connection as a player and
a Permitted one as the owner (the
copy-and-paste lookups' `exportActor` rule). Each tool is one `records.Lookups.Answer`
call, so privacy, the size caps (24 KB an answer) and page-walk limits are the
lookups'. `campaign_outline` is `Lookups.PageIndex` (8 KB); for the owner acting as the
owner it ends with the sidebar menu's category order (`Lookups.MenuOrder`) so the AI
can ask for a `menu-order` change. `initialize` tells the AI the tools only read and
that everything returned is the campaign's writing, never instructions. Every tool
except `ask_for_changes` is annotated `readOnlyHint: true`.

## Asking for changes

Only a connection made with "Ask for changes" lists the two extra tools.

- `how_to_write_changes` is the copy-and-paste prompt's standing instructions
  (`ai_workspace.Handler.ConnectorInstructions`: every block format, the rules, the
  campaign's page kinds and sheet fields, none of its content), in parts so each fits
  one read (`howto_parts.go`). Without arguments it is the guide, prefaced with what
  differs over a connection, with the sheet fields cut to a list of categories and the
  block formats cut to a pointer at `block_format`; `category` gives one category's
  fields and `part: categories` all of them. A guide under 24,000 characters (a
  player's) is answered whole. The split follows the prompt's `##` headings, pinned by
  `prompt/builder_test.go`.
- `ask_for_changes` takes `summary` (one line, 200 chars) and `changes` (the blocks, at
  most 256 KB and 200 blocks, lines kept). `Service.Ask` parses them as the review will
  and refuses, with the reason, a block that won't parse, a lookup or a part label, so
  the owner never gets a broken request; it names at most 5 problems and counts the
  rest. Then the campaign check (`SetCheck`, wired to
  `ai_workspace.Handler.ConnectorCheck`) refuses what the AI can fix itself, such as a
  new page filed in a folder its category lacks, or an update of a page that isn't there;
  a refusal is logged in "What it did" ("Asked to change Nobody Here, refused: no such page" for a page problem, which only an owner key is told; "Asked for changes, refused" otherwise) with the reasons,
  and nothing reaches the owner's panel; a failing check is logged and skipped
  (the review checks again). A good request is stored in `ai_connection_requests` with
  Chronicle's count ("2 to add, 1 to change") and nothing else happens; the AI is told
  nothing has changed. Annotated `readOnlyHint: false`, `destructiveHint: false`.
- The same changes (byte for byte) already waiting from the same person are not stored
  again (`ErrAlreadyWaiting`, checked in `Repository.AddRequest`'s transaction): the AI
  is told, without an error, that they are already waiting, so an app's retry after a
  timeout or an AI resending to be sure never leaves the owner two copies. Once
  answered, the same changes may be asked for again.
- At most `requestsWaiting` (20) requests wait per person per campaign
  (`Repository.AddRequest` counts and inserts in one transaction holding the campaign's
  row); the 21st is refused until they answer one. Answered ones are pruned to
  `requestsKept` (100) per person.
- An applied request's `outcome` (up to `outcomeMax`, 1000 chars) is the counts, then
  each change that failed or was held back with its reason
  (`ai_workspace.requestOutcome`); rows the owner answered No are only counted. The
  `waiting` lookup shows it to the AI, and says a turned-down request was the owner's
  choice, not an error.
- The reviewing side (`Waiting`, `WaitingRequest`, `Answered`, `TurnDown`) only reads or
  changes a request that came with the same person's key. Requests survive a
  disconnect: they are that person's to answer.

## Limits

- No per-address limit in front (hosted AI apps share addresses). Refused keys are
  limited per address (`failuresPerMinute` = 20, then 429 without a log line each); a
  good key always gets through. A key of the wrong shape never reaches the database.
  The first flood from an address also turns its wrong keys away for `blockFor` (24h,
  see "Watching for misuse").
- Per connection, in memory: tool calls come from a bucket of 30 that refills one a second, and 2000 a day (`callsBurst`, `callRefill`, `callsPerDay`). A call over the pace is answered as a tool result with `isError` saying how many seconds to wait (`waitText`), so the AI can retry rather than report a failure. Every request, handshakes and tool lists included, also has a looser bucket of 120 refilling two a second (`requestsBurst`, `requestsRefill`) in front, answered 429 with `Retry-After` and the seconds in the message.
- A request body is at most `maxBody` (576 KB: room for a 256 KB request for changes,
  escaped); one message per request (no batches); every other argument at most
  `argMax` (200 chars), and only a tool's own arguments are read: any other is refused with the list it takes (`readArgs`), so an AI never thinks a made-up filter was applied.
- A request with an `Origin` other than the site's own is refused (403).

## The call log

One row per `tools/call` plus a "Connected" row the first time an app connects: when,
tool, a one-line summary of what was asked (the AI's own words in quotes, so they never
read as Chronicle's), and a note (the answer's size, or why it wasn't answered). Never
the answer itself, and for a request for changes never the blocks ("Asked for 2
changes: “…”"). `CallsKept` (500) rows are kept per person per campaign; the page groups them into
sittings.

## Watching for misuse

Wired by `SetWatch` (`internal/app/routes.go`); nothing here runs without it.

- **Switches.** `Authenticate` checks, after the paused check, the server switch
  (`ai_server_access.ai_off`: 503 "AI is turned off on this server", `ErrAIOff`) and
  the campaign's cut-off (`ai_campaign_access.cut_off_at`: 403, `ErrCutOff`), read on
  every call. Neither revokes a key or touches waiting requests; turning back on lets
  every key in again. Players' keys also need the players' switches (below).
- **Warnings** (`ai_connection_alerts`), one open row per key (or per address when
  there is no key) and kind; a repeat adds to `hits`:
  - `new_app`: the key connected as an app name it had not given before
    (`NoteConnect` on `initialize`; `seen_apps` keeps the names, 255 chars). The first
    app is never a warning.
  - `limit`: the key was refused by its rate limit `limitAlertAt` (5) times since the
    server started (`NoteLimited`).
  - `wrong_keys`: an address passed `failuresPerMinute` (`NoteRefused`). It has no
    campaign, so only the site admin sees it.
- **Turn-away.** A `wrong_keys` warning also turns that address's wrong keys away for
  24h with 429 before any lookup. A working key from the same address still gets in,
  because hosted AI apps share addresses. The list is in memory and rebuilt from open
  `wrong_keys` warnings at start; settling the warning lifts it. The sync API's IP
  blocklist is not used, so Foundry sync can never be caught by it.
- **The owner** (Your AI page) sees their campaign's open warnings and answers each with
  "That was me" (`mine`) or "Pause the key" (`paused`, which also pauses the key and
  settles its other warnings), and has "Stop all AI here" (`SetCutOff`). All are
  owner-checked in the service.
- **The site admin** (`AdminView`, Site admin ▸ API & access ▸ AI apps) sees every live
  key with its campaign, maker, app and calls today, every open warning, the floods,
  and the last day's warnings; can pause, resume or end any key (`AdminKey`, settling
  its warnings), mark a warning looked at or lift a turn-away (`AdminSettle`), and set
  the server switches (`SetServerAccess`).

## Players' keys

A player makes their own key from their AI panel. It is off until both switches allow
it: the site admin's (`ai_server_access.players_allowed`, Site admin ▸ API & access ▸
AI apps) and the campaign owner's (`ai_campaign_access.players_allowed`, the Players' AI
panel on Your AI, `SetPlayersAllowed`, owner checked and refused while the admin blocks
players).

- `for_player` is fixed when the key is made: `Connect` makes a player's key whenever
  the maker isn't the owner (`Owners.CheckMember`, then `checkPlayerSwitches`). Its
  rights come from that column, never from the maker's role at call time, so a key made
  as a player stays a player's key after an ownership handover. A scribe or co-GM who
  makes a key from the player panel gets a player's key.
- Privacy is always Safe; `Authenticate` checks membership (`ErrPlayerGone` once they
  leave) and the switches on every call, failing closed: no switches wired, or a read
  error, refuses the key (`ErrPlayersOff`, `ErrPlayersBlocked`, 403 with the reason).
- Tools marked `Owner` (the Director's tools: rolling tables, Foundry scenes and
  monsters, waiting for review, players, encounters, tests) are not listed and not
  callable; tools with `PlayerDesc` describe themselves for a player.
  `how_to_write_changes` and the campaign check come from
  `ai_workspace.Handler.ConnectorInstructions` and `ConnectorCheck`, which branch on the
  key: a player's AI may only send `action: update` blocks for a character the player
  claimed, changing sheet fields their sheet editor allows.
- The owner sees each player's live key (`PlayerKeys`: player, app, last used, calls
  today) and can pause, resume (`StopPlayerKey`) or disconnect it (`EndPlayerKey`), never
  what it read or asked. `EndCampaignKeys` ends every key in a campaign when it moves
  to Trash; on a handover the old owner's key stops at its next call (`Owners.Check`).
