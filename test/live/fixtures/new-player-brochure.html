Therin: Shattered Heroes

A fantasy campaign for Draw Steel. Everything in here is safe to read before you join.

What you'd be playing

You play someone with a rare power: one of the few people in the world who can fight monsters and win. The story starts with a dangerous job and a handful of strangers, and your hero ends up among them. How they got there is up to you. Maybe they chose it, maybe they were forced into it, maybe they stumbled in by accident.

Who they were before is yours to decide too. They might be a farmhand nobody has heard of, a soldier with a reputation, or a local favourite with a following.

The game is about what happens next. Expect danger, mystery, hard choices and a party of strangers slowly becoming something more. Expect to leave home and not come back for a long time.

The world: Therin

Therin is an old, dangerous world, and Saltreach is where it starts. It has three moons, many gods and more ruins than anyone can count. Most people live behind walls, because the land between towns is wild, and the wild is full of things that magic has twisted into monsters. Travelling between cities means accepting that you might not arrive.

A hundred years ago, something broke the world. People call it the Shattering. The world has been slowly rebuilding ever since, and the age since then is known as the Hollow. Your story starts in the year 100 of the Hollow.

Magic is real but mostly small: a village healer, a temple blessing. Big magic is rare, and the world has paid dearly for it before. The gods are real too. They are many, they don't always agree, and who worships whom changes from place to place.

Where you start: Saltreach

Saltreach is a walled port city on the far southern coast of a land called Imix. It makes its living from salt, which caravans haul out of the dry country behind the city. The locals even name their coins after salt.

Power in the city is balanced, and nobody is fully in charge:

  • The Warden's watch holds the walls.
  • The caravans move the salt and the money.
  • The temples keep shrines in Temple Square to Lacrimosa, Aðr, Sekhar and Memoria, and a sailors' altar to Ilune on the docks.
  • The Adventurer's Guild hires people with power like yours.

What your hero is

Sparks and the Ignited

Your hero's power comes from a Spark. Sparks are rare, they look like gemstones, and each one is different. Binding one is called being Ignited. People without a Spark are called Barren, and most people are Barren.

Once bound, a Spark turns into a power that fits the person who holds it. One person's Spark makes them a deadly fighter, another's lets them call on a god, another's bends fire and stone. Because every Spark is unique, nobody in the world talks about "classes". At the table, though, your Draw Steel class is simply the closest fit for what your Spark became.

How the Ignited grow

The Ignited grow stronger through real, hard experience. The body sets a limit, though: a Spark can't pour in more power than its holder is ready for. Each step up is harder than the last, and most Ignited stall somewhere along the way. At the table, heroes level up as normal in Draw Steel.

The Adventurer's Guild

The Ignited are the only real answer to the monsters, so the Adventurer's Guild hires them. It is one organisation with halls in many cities. It hands out contracts (clear a road, find the missing, kill the thing in the cave) and ranks its members by how strong they've grown. The ranks match Draw Steel's four tiers of play, called echelons.

Rank What Saltreach calls it Echelon Levels
Copper "Rock" 1st 1 to 3. Most members. A level 1 hero would rank here.
Silver "Brine" 2nd 4 to 6. Veterans.
Gold "Fleur" 3rd 7 to 9. A handful on each continent.
Starsteel "White" 4th 10. Near-legendary.

Two more you'll hear about:

  • Iron is a separate rank for people without a Spark who are skilled enough to do the same work.
  • The Legends are five top heroes who lead the whole Guild. You can't apply. They invite you.

Everyday life

  • Technology is late medieval, with magic filling the gaps. There is no gunpowder, no electricity, and printing is rare. Ships run on sail.
  • Money is mostly flavour in Draw Steel, but here are the names you'll hear.

Crown gold a sack contracts, horses, bribes
  • Death is serious. When someone dies, their soul lingers near the body for a few days, then fades. Bringing someone back is extremely rare, hard even for gods, and never cheap. Don't count on it.

How this campaign plays

  • You start small. Whatever your past, your power is at its first step, and you don't know the other heroes yet.
  • The world is against you. Monsters, magic, gods and worse. Safety is something you find in the big cities, not on the road.
  • The campaign travels far. Build a hero who is able to leave Saltreach behind.
  • Players drive. You decide what your hero does, and the world answers with consequences and rewards.
  • Bring one loose end. Every hero at the table has something after them. Give the DM a thread to pull.

Making your hero

  1. Build in Chronicle, the campaign's website. The DM will send you an invite, and the "Making a hero" box on the home page walks you through it. Every class is open: Beastheart, Censor, Conduit, Elementalist, Fury, Null, Shadow, Summoner, Tactician, Talent and Troubadour.
  2. Start at level 1, the same as the party.
  3. Talk to the DM about your ancestry before writing a backstory. Any ancestry in the Draw Steel book is open, plus the Fairy. But the peoples of Therin are not the ones described in the book, so treat the book's write-up as rules only. Pick the one whose abilities you like, then ask the DM what that people is like here.
  4. Pick your class, and your Spark follows. You and the DM decide what your Spark looks like together. Balanced homebrew flavour is welcome.
  5. Your past is open. Unknown or famous, rich or poor. Anything that fits a level 1 hero works.
  6. If your class serves a god (a Conduit or Censor, for example), ask the DM who fits. The Saltreach shrines are a good place to start.
  7. Answer four questions, briefly:
    • What does your Spark look like, and where do you wear it?
    • When and where did you bind it?
    • How did you end up on this job (by choice, by force or by accident), and what do you want out of it?
    • What's your loose end: who or what is after you?
  8. Write your own intro. Bring a one-minute "how my hero walks in". The DM will work out with you how you meet the others.

The table

  • We're a small group of friends who talk openly at the table.
  • Before your first session we'll agree lines and veils together. Lines are things that never appear in the game. Veils are things that can happen, but off screen. Bring yours.
  • Anyone can pause the game at any time, no reason needed.
  • The DM keeps secrets for the story. If something feels hidden, it probably is, and you'll find it in play.
